Hi there, I'm Jeremy — credentials, achievements & mission

Six treatments for the founder intro on jiki-landing.html, all built on the same three parts: a row of credentials (who he is), a row of achievements (what came of it), and eight sentences on his mission and how he teaches. The two rows get deliberately different treatments in every idea — a reader skimming should be able to tell that the first is a CV and the second is a result. Content is identical throughout; only the treatment changes.

Credentials — who he is

  • Senior developer
  • 20 years experience
  • AI background
  • Founder of Exercism

Achievements — what came of it

  • 2M+ people taught
  • 40+ countries reached
  • Free, forever
1

Quiet tags, loud numbers

The credentials sit small and grey — a CV line, not a claim. The achievements get the stat-rail treatment underneath: 34px figures with hairline dividers, so "2M+" is the largest thing in the block rather than the smallest. Same figure treatment the Exercism band lower down the page already uses, so the two moments of proof speak alike.

Hi there! 👋 I'm Jeremy, and I've helped over two million people level up their coding skills.

Senior developer 20 years experience AI background Founder of Exercism
2M+people taught
40+countries reached
Freeforever, for everyone

I've been writing software for twenty years and I still think it's the most fun you can have with a keyboard. I've built companies that worked, a couple that spectacularly didn't, and founded Exercism — a not-for-profit that has now taught over two million people to code, for free. What all of that taught me is that nobody learns to code by watching someone else do it. So Jiki doesn't hand you answers: it hands you a problem just past what you can already do, then stays next to you while you work it out. When you get stuck it tells you what your code actually did, in plain English, instead of leaving you to decode an error message. I'm not trying to get you through a quiz — I want you to be the person your friends call when their laptop is on fire. Programming gave me a career, a livelihood and a genuinely brilliant twenty years making things. I'd very much like you to have that too.

— Jeremy
